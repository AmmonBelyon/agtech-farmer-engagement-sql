# agtech-farmer-engagement-sql
## SQL-driven analytics case study mapping user engagement and localized churn across 500+ emerging market smallholder farmers to inform AgTech product design and regional SDG 2/8 policy interventions.

### Exploratory Data Findings & Regional Analysis

### Insight 1: Smallholder Attrition & Retention Barriers

**The Data:** Central Region experiences the highest critical drop-off at 88.89% churn, followed closely by the Rift Valley (76.17%) and Nyanza (75.38%).

**The Policy Interpretation:** Central and Rift Valley have high concentrations of users but struggle with long-term retention. Conversely, the Eastern Region shows a lower mathematical churn rate (52.17%). However, cross-referencing this with Insight 3 reveals that Eastern also has the absolute lowest transaction counts (only 85 input purchases), proving that structural network connection issues or severe low-adoption gaps in Eastern mask active churn metrics.

### Insight 2: Crop Payout Efficiencies (SDG 2 Progress)

**The Data:** Eastern Beans yield the highest financial efficiency on the platform at KES 10,630.28 per hectare, closely followed by Nyanza Potatoes (KES 10,390.15/Ha).

**The Policy Interpretation:** While corporate agricultural support heavily favors primary grain distribution networks like Rift Valley Maize, micro-interventions focused on high-yielding legume and root crops (like Beans and Potatoes) return more liquid cash back to smallholders per unit of land, directly supporting household economic resilience.

### Insight 3: FinTech Capital Velocity (SDG 8 Progress)

**The Data:** Rift Valley dominates platform marketplace liquidity with 490 input purchases totaling KES 2,371,982 in digital seed/fertilizer investments. Average input costs remain remarkably flat across all regions (~KES 4,800 to KES 4,900 per transaction).

**The Policy Interpretation:** Capital velocity is highly concentrated where agricultural plots are structurally larger (Rift Valley). FinTech tools face a scalability gap when moving into regions with highly fragmented, smaller farm sizes (like Eastern), necessitating zero-rated transaction fees or subsidized group-buying features within the mobile app framework to spark adoption.
